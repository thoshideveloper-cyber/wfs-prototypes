The situation

India makes more films than any country on earth. And no way in.

There are four ways to learn filmmaking in this city. Spend several lakh rupees and four years at an institute. Take a one-off workshop that teaches a fragment and ends. Buy an online course you will not finish. Or know somebody. Three of those are unavailable to a person with a job. The fourth is the entire problem.

The professional

Watches an enormous amount of cinema. Has wanted to make something for six years. Has a phone that shoots 4K and free editing software already installed, and has never made anything, because a film needs five other people and a deadline, and neither is available to buy.

24 to 35 · Employed

The actor

Trained, unseen. Needs footage to be cast, and needs to be cast to get footage. The entire showreel is a bathroom mirror and one wedding video.

Trained · Unseen

The writer

Has ideas, has pages, has nobody to make them with. Every script sits in a folder, because writing is the only part of filmmaking you can do alone, and it is the part that needs everyone else.

Fourteen drafts deep

What you are missing is not knowledge. It is a crew, a deadline, a room, and somebody to watch it.

None of those four are available for sale anywhere in this city. So we sell exactly those four things, packaged as one Saturday.

10 AM to 7 PM

What one Saturday actually looks like

Nine hours, four blocks, one finished film. The schedule is not aspirational. The export at 6:15 is a hard stop, and it is the only reason anybody finishes.

10 AM to 12

Learn

Story, framing, sound, and the three shots every scene needs. Then crews are drawn from a hat. An actual hat.

12:30 to 4:15

Shoot

Lunch, then a two-page script, locked. Then shooting, all within 200 metres. Nobody sits in traffic.

4:30 to 6:15

Cut

Edit, sound, music, titles with everyone's name in them. Hard export at 6:15. Somebody always asks for ten more minutes.

6:30 to 7:15

Premiere

Everyone watches everything. Applause after every single film. At least one person films the screen.

The rule that makes it work

Everything is shot within two hundred metres of the venue. The cafe, the terrace, the stairwell, the street outside.

It sounds like a limitation. It is the only reason the film gets finished by six, and it forces a real scene instead of a montage of establishing shots.

Before you arrive

Five short lessons, forty minutes each, included in the price. Story, frame, sound, performance, cut.
